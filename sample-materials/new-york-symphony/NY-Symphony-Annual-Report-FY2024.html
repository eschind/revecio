A Letter from Leadership

Dear Friends,

Fiscal 2024 was a year of artistic ambition and financial progress for The New York Symphony. We welcomed more than 320,000 listeners to Hudson Concert Hall and venues across the city, presented two world premieres, and carried New York’s sound to audiences on our first international tour since the pandemic. Behind the music, the institution grew stronger: we closed the year with a modest operating surplus, our endowment reached a new high of $186 million, and our Centennial Campaign surpassed $61 million toward its $75 million goal.

None of this is possible without you. Earned revenue covers less than half the cost of presenting live orchestral music; the rest is a gift from a community that believes, as we do, that music belongs to everyone. Thank you for making this season possible — and for helping us build a foundation that will carry the Symphony into its second century.

Eleanor Vance, Chair of the Board  ·  Marcus Bell, President & CEO  ·  Sofia Klein, Music Director

On the Stage

The 2023–24 season spanned the canon and the contemporary. Music Director Sofia Klein led acclaimed cycles of Brahms and Sibelius, while the composer-in-residence program brought two world premieres to the Hudson stage. The Symphony’s spring tour visited four European capitals to standing ovations and strong press, reaffirming the orchestra’s international standing. The pops and family series played to near-capacity houses, and the free Concerts in the Parks drew more than 50,000 New Yorkers across the five boroughs.

In the Community

  • Symphony in the Schools partnered with 140 public schools, embedding teaching artists in classrooms across all five boroughs.
  • Young People’s Concerts introduced more than 40,000 students to live orchestral music.
  • The new Hudson Fellowship welcomed its first cohort of early-career musicians from historically underrepresented backgrounds.

Financial Summary

The Symphony ended FY2024 with total operating revenue and support of $49.7 million against operating expenses of $47.1 million, producing a $2.6 million operating surplus. Strong investment returns and campaign gifts to the endowment lifted total net assets to $237.0 million.

Endowment & the Centennial Campaign

The endowment is the Symphony’s financial backbone, providing reliable support that lets us keep tickets accessible and sustain education programs through every economic cycle. In FY2024 the endowment grew to $186 million, aided by $12.5 million in new campaign gifts and net investment returns. The Centennial Campaign has now raised $61 million toward its $75 million goal, of which $40 million is designated to grow the endowment. As the endowment grows, the Board is reviewing its investment governance to ensure the portfolio is managed with the discipline and sophistication the institution’s future deserves.
